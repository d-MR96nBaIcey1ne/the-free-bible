# The Ultra Shelf — Twenty Stones

**The current version of every Ultra Bloom. One file each. Working documents, not finished works.**

An *Ultra* is a full treatment of a passage: the source language set verbatim from a named witness, a rendering bloomed from that source rather than adapted from an existing translation, a before-text for comparison, and — beside every verse — the recoveries. What the source says that English cannot carry, what was cut, and what was reached for and put back.

Nothing here is finalised. **The stones stay open.** As the method tightens, earlier stones are brought forward: the Shema was bloomed in July and never chiselled, and was verified, cut and rebuilt six weeks later. That is the intended lifecycle, not a repair.

---

## The method these demonstrate

**The two-hand rule.** Blooming and chiselling are done by *different hands*. One model scaffolds the structure cold, from the source, with the renderings left empty. A second model blooms into that scaffold. The first returns and chisels once. A hand cannot audit its own reach.

**Both-ways sequencing.** Every part is scaffolded before any part is bloomed, and every part is bloomed before any part is chiselled. Contamination runs in both directions — and a ring cannot be verified by inspecting one arc.

**Cut what explains; keep what recovers.** A gloss standing inside the rendering is cut, because the reader cannot tell which words are the text. A genuine lexical recovery is kept, however strange it reads. Where the two conflict, the refusal is printed beside the cut.

**Two witnesses.** The source is fetched, stored verbatim, and derived programmatically — never transcribed by hand. It is then verified word for word against a second independent edition. Divergences are recorded rather than smoothed. Ketiv/qere forks are declared at the verse where they fall. Where a witness cannot supply cantillation, the gap is declared and not filled.

---

## What you are looking at

These are **stones from a quarry**, not a readers' edition. The chisel notes stay. The refusals stay. The record of how many passes a stone took, and which hand made which cut, stays. A finished reader would not want any of it — but that is not who these are for yet.

The framework is the point. **Here is the architecture; run it on your own model.**
