## 0. WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS

Every Ultra built before this specification took its shape from the stone built before it. No stone referenced a written standard, because none existed. That produced a **clone chain**: each build inherited the previous build's decisions, including its faults, and nobody decided anything.

The shelf-wide sweep of September 2026 found the evidence. Every stone to 28 July uses bulleted source recovery; every stone from 29 July uses prose. One clean version boundary, with no ruling behind it — the format changed because one builder did it differently and eleven stones copied.

**A template perpetuates a clone chain. A specification ends one.**

From this document forward, a new Ultra references *this file*. It does not reference the previous stone.

**Stones built before this specification keep the format they were built in.** The prose-recovery stones stand as pre-specification artifacts. They may be revisited; they are not retrofitted by default.

## 1. WHAT AN ULTRA IS

An Ultra is a **full-passage stone**: a psalm, a canticle, or a long passage treated at depth. It is distinguished from a Prayer-of-the-Day tablet by scope, not by method.

- **POD** — a single verse, one page, one bloom face, built same-day.
- **Ultra** — a whole passage, multi-section document, built over one or more sittings, carrying apparatus a tablet cannot hold.

If a passage fits on one tablet face and needs no apparatus, it is a POD.

**An Ultra ships as .docx, never as a PDF. [v1.6]** The stones stay open (§0), so none is issued as a PDF: its PDF render exists for the asserts alone (§5), and a single-image render follows §4.4. A POD is finalised, and ships as .docx, PDF and JPG. Ruled by the house, 7 October 2026.

## 2. THE CANONICAL SECTION ORDER

The order below is canonical. **Every section is required except the Round-Trip, which is the only conditional section.** **[v1.7]** A Prayer to Say (§2.8A) is required on every stone built from version 1.7; a stone built earlier gains one only when it is revisited. Multi-verse stones follow §2A within this order.

### 2.1 Masthead
- Reference line — *Book Chapter:Verse Ultra*
- Stone name — the crown, not the famous phrase
- One-line gloss — what this stone is for
- Date of build — on a staged build, the date of each sitting (§2A) **[v1.3]**
- Place — *Newgate, Western Australia*
- The chamber line — *A Free Bible chamber-tradition document*
- Optional context line — one sentence situating the book

### 2.2 Foreword
Three sub-blocks, in this order:
- **Why this exists** — the reason this passage earned a stone
- **Structure** — what layers the document carries, and the textual base used
- **A discipline line** — how the hands were kept separate on this build

### 2.3 The Face
- Reference heading
- **The English (translation named)** — the received rendering, with its version identified
- **Bloomed Rendering** — the face, lineated

### 2.4 The Source
- Heading — *The Hebrew Source* or *The Greek Source*; the label names the script present (assert 3)
- The source text, **single line per verse**, with every layer the script carries (Annex A, Annex B)
- The transliteration, **single line per verse**

**Nothing else may stand between the heading and the transliteration.** See §4.1.

### 2.5 Source Recovery
- Heading — *Source recovery:*
- **Bullets**, **contiguous**, one per recovered element. Bullets are the house format from this specification forward.
- Each bullet: transliteration, the source word in parentheses, an em-dash, the recovery in plain English, with the crown word in capitals
- **Declarations live here** — textual variants, normalisations, pointing questions, and anything the house chose rather than found

### 2.6 The Recovery
A prose paragraph stating what the passage is doing, once the recoveries are in hand. This is the argument of the stone.

### 2.7 The Bloomed Verse · Entire
- Heading, with a use-line beneath it
- **The Source · The Verse in One Stone** — the source broken into sense-groups
- The transliteration, broken to match
- The English face, broken to match

**The three blocks must mirror each other line for line.**

### 2.8 The Round-Trip [conditional]
- Heading — *The Bloom Rendered Back — An Experiment*
- A line stating plainly that this is an indicative draft for shape-comparison, not a finalised text
- The re-composed source

### 2.8A A Prayer to Say [v1.7]
The stone answers itself once, in the reader's voice. The Prayers of Devotion on the repository define the form: *a short prayer that articulates what the bloom has revealed, written in first-person form for the reader to speak aloud.* It answers the passage; it is not part of the rendering and adds nothing to it, which is how an Ultra, the re-rendering and study of what is already there, can carry it. With it on the stone, a Prayer of Devotion can be drawn from an Ultra rather than written apart from it.
- **Place.** After the Bloomed Verse · Entire, or the Bloomed Passage · Entire on a multi-verse stone (§2A), and after the Round-Trip where a stone has one; before any dated entry and before the Closing.
- **Heading** — *A Prayer to Say*, set as the stone's other section headings.
- **One line beneath it,** in the voice of the stone's notes: *Not part of the rendering: a short prayer in the first person, answering what the bloom revealed, for the reader to say aloud.*
- **The prayer,** lineated and centred, in bold and not italic, at the size of the English face, every line whole on its line, the block held together on one page (§4.5).
- **Its text** keeps the face's conventions (§3.8): the Name as the face writes it; ordinary sentence capitals, *you* and never *You*; no quotation marks and no possessive apostrophes (assert 9); no dash as a breath mark. About four to eight lines. It answers what the bloom revealed and may reach into the scripture the stone itself points to; it brings in nothing the stone did not find.
- **Its hands.** Written by the two-Fable chain (§6), and named (§2.9).
- **A stone built earlier** gains its prayer only when it is revisited, as a change after the seal (§2.9), stone by stone, never in a sweep of the shelf. Psalm 131, *My Soul Upon Me*, is the first, 14 October 2026.

### 2.9 Closing and Colophon
- Where *The English* (§2.3) is a copyrighted translation quoted at length, the colophon carries that publisher's required permission notice. **[v1.3]**
- **The seal, last. [v1.6]** At finalisation the part markers and the checking notes come out (§2A), the credit names every hand, and the seal goes in as the last paragraph: `SealM-master.png` from the repository (§4.4), 1.8 inches wide and centred, as on Ultra 21, held with the credit and the date of the seal (§4.5). A seal marks a stone finished between the chambers. It does not close it: the stones stay open (§0).
- **A change after the seal. [v1.6]** It is made on the sealed file as it stands on the repository, its hash checked, never on a rebuild, and it is recorded in a dated entry just before the Closing, a line for a small change and a headed cutting for a larger one, saying what moved, why, and that nothing else did. The seal and its date stand as they were, and the seal stays the last thing in the stone. Psalm 131 records its revision of 12 October 2026 this way, and Psalm 121 its cuttings.
- **The hands of the prayer to say. [v1.7]** The credit names them as it names the bloom's. On a stone that gains its prayer after the seal, the dated entry names them, and the credit stands as it was, as on Psalm 131.

## 2A. MULTI-VERSE STONES

The Zephaniah stone is a single verse, and §2 was written from it. A long passage keeps the same order with these adaptations.

- **The verse is the unit.** A source panel never spans a verse boundary. The sof pasuq, or the verse number in Greek, closes each panel.
- **Each verse carries its own cycle**: verse reference, the received English for that verse, face lines, source line, transliteration, and that verse's recovery bullets.
- **The English moves into the cycle. [v1.5]** Each verse cycle carries its own received rendering, labelled with its translation, between the verse reference and the Bloomed Rendering, so every verse reads before and after. The passage is never also printed whole: §2.3's single English block belongs to single-verse stones only. The shelf has always done it this way — Psalm 121, Psalm 139, Isaiah 40 — and the first scaffold built from the specification did not, because the specification never said so.
- **Uneven verse lengths need no special handling.** Each verse is lineated by its own structure (§3.7): a long verse breaks at the atnach and then the lesser disjunctives; a short verse at the atnach alone — or, where it carries no atnach, at the fallback in §3.7. **[v1.3]** What counts as long is the stone's declared threshold (§3.7). **[v1.4]**
- **Strophes become headings**, where the passage has them — an acrostic's letters, the paired times of Ecclesiastes 3. They group verses. They never merge panels. **Where the witness marks a paragraph division inside the passage, the strophes follow it [v1.4]** — derived from the witness like the lineation, never chosen by eye. The Leningrad Codex closes Ecclesiastes 3:1 and 3:8 with a setumah, so the Masoretes themselves set the fourteen pairs apart (Annex A).
- **The Recovery (§2.6) is written once**, for the whole passage.
- **§2.7 becomes *The Bloomed Passage · Entire***, assembled as on the shelf **[v1.5]**: three continuous blocks for the whole passage — the source broken into its lines, then the transliteration broken to match, then the English face as one prayer to be spoken, with a 5pt stanza blank at each strophe boundary in all three blocks, so the blocks still mirror line for line (assert 6). No verse numbers or labels interrupt the blocks. The line-for-line mirror is still enforced per verse, by the build rather than on the page. (v1.2 to v1.4 said *with verse numbers*; the shelf never did, and the house ruled for the shelf.)
- **The Round-Trip, if used, is attempted on one chosen verse**, not the whole passage.
- **Staged builds. [v1.3]** Where the bloom must be split across sittings, the house's both-ways sequencing governs: **scaffold all parts before any bloom; bloom all parts before any chisel; the chisel runs once**, over the whole. The scaffold — masthead, source panels, transliteration, and every verse cycle with its face and bullet slots left empty — is built whole, in one pass; only the bloom is divided. A part never splits a verse, and part boundaries follow strophes where the passage has them. §2.6 and §2.7 are written after the last part is bloomed.
- **The scaffold's conventions. [v1.4]** Every slot is bracketed and names what fills it. Face slots are laid out line for line against the source's lineation, in each verse cycle and again in §2.7, so the bloom inherits the accent breaks rather than inventing its own. A marker line opens each bloom part. Declarations the builder finds in the witness — paragraph markers, ketiv and qere, variants — enter that verse's recovery block as its first bullets, ahead of the bloom's slot, so the run stays contiguous (§4.2). Notes for the checking chamber stand at the end of the document and are removed at finalisation.

## 3. TEXTUAL RULES

### 3.1 Never hand-transcribe source text
Fetch it, store it verbatim, derive every line programmatically, verify against the source. Three consecutive undercounts on earlier stones came from typing.

**This includes every string an assert compares against. [v1.3]** Assert code derives its expected values from the witness and never types a source string. A typed Hebrew key failed on codepoint order in the Exodus 20:8 POD build, and was caught only because it raised an error rather than passing.

**The received English is a witness too. [v1.4]** The rendering quoted in §2.3 is fetched, from two independent sources where two exist, stored verbatim, and asserted against the page (assert 15). For Ecclesiastes 3 the BSB was taken from a public corpus on GitHub and confirmed against API.Bible.

**Witnesses are fetched fresh for each build. [v1.4]** A copy that has gone missing is fetched again, never reconstructed. The working directory was wiped between two builds on 5 October 2026, and nothing was lost, because nothing depended on a cached copy.

### 3.2 The textual base is named
State which text the stone follows, and declare every fork.
- **Hebrew** — the **Westminster Leningrad Codex** (Annex A).
- **Greek** — the **critical text**; Textus Receptus and Byzantine readings are declared in the recovery bullets (Annex B).

### 3.3 The manuscript form stands; the normalisation is declared
Where the manuscript carries an unexpected reading, **the source panels carry the manuscript form** and the recovery block carries a declaration. The normalisation was never wrong — only undeclared.

### 3.4 Script layers belong to received text only
Source panels carry every layer of the received text. A round-trip panel is a **composition**, not a received text, and carries no cantillation. The divergence between them is the point, not a cost — it marks the distinction visually. Script-specific layers are listed in the annexes.

### 3.5 The palimpsest rule
Where a word admits more than one reading, **print all of them**. Name the one the stone takes, and name it as a choice. Record declined readings as *declined, not unknown*.

### 3.6 The copula ruling
No verb anywhere in the verse means the verblessness is the theology and is kept. A verb present elsewhere makes a verbless clause ordinary nominal idiom and the copula is supplied. **What matters is predication, not the presence of a verb tag** — a substantival participle predicates nothing.

### 3.7 Lineation follows the source's structure
Hebrew breaks at the atnach, then the disjunctives. Greek breaks on its clause structure (Annex B). Do not break on English sense against the source's structure.

**Where a verse carries no atnach [v1.3]**, its major division falls at its highest-ranking remaining disjunctive: in the prose books, the segolta or zaqef; the tipcha only when nothing higher stands. The worked cases are Ecclesiastes 3:3, 4 and 6–8, which divide their two pairs at the zaqef, and Exodus 20:8, which divides at the tipcha. The three poetic books follow their own table, below. **The build derives every break from the accents; none is chosen by eye.**

**The poetic books. [v1.6]** Psalms, Proverbs and Job carry their own accent system. The house's working order is the order in which the *Hebrew Grammar* of Gesenius lists their disjunctives, in its section 15: the oleh ve-yored; then the atnach; then revia gadol, revia mugrash and great shalshelet; then tsinnor; then revia qatan and dechi; then pazer, mehuppakh legarmeh and azla legarmeh. The silluq closes every verse, so the oleh ve-yored is the strongest pause inside one; where a verse also carries an atnach, the atnach usually divides the half that follows. Both divide a verse unconditionally, as the atnach does in prose, so a verse carrying both breaks at each; a verse carrying neither takes the fallback above, read with this table. Psalm 131 is the worked case: the order was declared on its scaffold for the house to rule, set right at the Free Quarry's check of the scaffold, which put the oleh ve-yored above the atnach, and carried by the stone to its seal.

**The ranks, and ties. [v1.4]** The house's working order for the prose books is: atnach; then segolta, shalshelet and zaqef; then tipcha; then revia; then the remaining disjunctives. Where a stretch of verse holds more than one disjunctive of the winning rank, it breaks at each of them.

**When a line breaks again. [v1.4]** Each stone declares one threshold, in words, and applies it to every verse: any line longer than the threshold breaks again at its own highest-ranking disjunctive, until every line fits. The threshold is chosen so that no line stands as a single word. It is declared in the scaffold notes and carried into the foreword's Structure block at finalisation. Ecclesiastes 3 takes eight: at six, 3:14 left *yadaʿti* and *hu* standing alone. **The count is of graphic units [v1.6]:** a maqqef-joined group counts as one word and a paseq not at all, as Ultra 21 and Psalm 131 both counted. Psalm 131 takes four.

### 3.8 House conventions
- **YHWH renders the tetragrammaton, and only the tetragrammaton.** Where the Hebrew has the Name, the face carries YHWH, never *the LORD*. Where the Greek has θεός, the face carries *God*. **The convention follows the source, not the house preference** — Philippians 1:3 and Psalm 9:1, on consecutive days, one on each side of the line.
- **κύριος** — stays *Lord*, never small-capital *LORD*. Where κύριος stands for YHWH in a quotation of the Hebrew scriptures, the recovery bullets say so; the face does not convert it.
- No Latin punctuation in any source panel.
- No quotation marks on any face or panel.
- **Capitals** — emphasis capitals mark the crown word in recovery bullets only. Bloom faces use ordinary sentence capitals and carry no emphasis capitals.
- **Face punctuation. [v1.6]** A face is punctuated as English prayer, in the style of Ecclesiastes 3 and Psalm 131, and its line-end marks follow the breaks the accents set (§3.7): a full stop at the end of the verse; a semicolon at its major division, which is the atnach, the oleh ve-yored in the poetic books, or, where a verse has neither, the division §3.7 falls back to, as in Ecclesiastes 3:3, 4 and 6–8; a comma at a lesser break. English sense governs where it differs. A question closes on its question mark (Ecclesiastes 3:9); a clause that opens onto the next takes a colon (3:13); a lesser break that closes a full clause may take a semicolon (3:14), and a major one inside a running sentence a comma (3:12); a line that runs on into the next takes no mark at all (3:10, 3:11). Inside a line, a comma may mark a strong pause the accents set there (Psalm 131:1 and 2). A superscription standing as its own line closes with a full stop (131:1). A dash stands on a face only to hold a clause whose verb the Hebrew leaves out and the face keeps out (§3.6), as in *For everything — a season* (Ecclesiastes 3:1); never round a gloss, and never as a breath mark. Ruled by the house, 13 October 2026. Stones built earlier keep their marks (§0).

## 4. LAYOUT RULES

### 4.1 A source panel contains source and nothing else
Source text and its transliteration. **No bullet, no note, no parenthesis, no commentary.** Anything the house says about the text belongs where the house speaks — the recovery block.

This is the general rule. It covers cases nobody has thought of yet.

### 4.2 Recovery bullets are contiguous
No prose may interrupt the bullet run.

### 4.3 Spacers mark sense-group boundaries
Blank paragraphs set to **5pt**, never inherited body size. Count must equal `len(groups) - 1` exactly. Strip all blanks before inserting. This governs Ultra documents; a POD face follows the template's stanza blanks instead (§4.4). **[v1.3]**

### 4.4 Single-image renders
This document governs an Ultra's content and structure. The Prayer-of-the-Day geometry — uniform margins, single-page crop, seal count, width and aspect bounds — is not part of it. **When an Ultra is rendered as a single tablet image, as the Romans Ultra was, the POD house specification governs that render.**

The POD canonical template lives in the public repository at `Prayers-for-Life/Prayers-of-Day/Ultra-Prayer-of-the-Day-Tablet-Template-v2.docx`, with the seal master beside it (`SealM-master.png`). The template carries its own written format specification after the tablet; the build keeps the tablet and drops those pages. Every POD build and every single-image render starts from it — never from the previous stone. **[v1.3]**

**The template governs. [v1.3]** Where the template and a register, a recent stone, or this document differ, the template wins — restore it in full rather than reconciling piecemeal. One change compensated elsewhere is how drift hides. The restoration of 4 October 2026 found exactly that: the closing stanza blank had been removed and the source label's space raised to cover the gap, the source type had grown from 13pt to 14pt, and the Hebrew lines had lost their right-to-left direction. On a POD face the template closes every stanza with a blank, the last included, so a POD carries `len(groups)` stanza blanks at the template's 8pt; §4.3 governs Ultra documents only. The one value the template cannot hold is page height: its fixed 11-inch page is kept, and extended only when a stone needs more, as the longest stones do.

### 4.5 Pagination [v1.6]
Pages break where the renderer puts them. The document says only what must not be parted, by keep-with-next, never by a page break or blank padding. On an Ultra:
- every heading and label keeps with what it heads, so none stands at the foot of a page, a dated cutting or revision line included;
- a face keeps together, every line but its last keeping with the next, and a source panel keeps with its transliteration;
- each block of §2.7 keeps together, its reading note and label with it;
- a round-trip keeps together, its draft line, its lines and its note;
- a prayer to say keeps together, its heading, the line beneath it, and every line of the prayer but the last **[v1.7]**;
- the credit, the date of the seal and the seal hold together.

The Free Quarry found four Psalm 121 cutting headings without it, and the stone took it on 12 October 2026. Psalm 131 was paginated this way at the chisel.

**A heading is found by its place, never by its weight. [v1.7]** The prayer to say is set in bold and its lines are not headings. The Psalm 131 build read every bold paragraph as a heading, and had to be read through the insertion when the prayer went in.

## 5. THE BUILD ASSERTS

Every assert below was earned from a failure. Run all of them on the final artifact, in the form the reader will see.

1. **Word-for-word diff** against the fetched witness — identical token lists.
2. **Order assert** — not only multiset. Two identical strings in one passage will pass a multiset check while sitting in the wrong places.
3. **Label-script match** — detect the script present, require the heading to name it. A Greek label must never sit over Hebrew.
4. **Layer check** — every layer of the script survives docx to PDF: letters, vowels and cantillation for Hebrew (Annex A); letters, accents and breathings for Greek (Annex B). **Like for like [v1.4]:** every character of the script in the file against every character of the script in the PDF — never the panels against the whole PDF. Two declaration bullets quoting ס read as two phantom letters in the first scaffold build.
5. **Meteg filter** (Hebrew) — U+05BD is a **fourth layer**. Strip it on *both sides* of every comparison. Unfiltered, it reports a phantom divergence and nobody knows which line is real.
6. **Spacer count** — blanks in each block equal `len(groups) - 1`.
7. **Zero U+FFFD**; source-language survival into the PDF.
8. **No Latin punctuation in any source-bearing paragraph.** Greek orthographic marks are not punctuation (Annex B).
9. **No quotation marks anywhere.** The check cannot tell a possessive apostrophe from a closing single quote — both are U+2019 — so a stone's own prose writes around possessives rather than weakening the check. **[v1.4]** It caught one in the Ecclesiastes scaffold's foreword.
10. **The LORD must never appear**, outside the received English, which is a witness and keeps its translators' *LORD* (assert 15). **[v1.6]** The match is case-sensitive, so a Greek face's *the Lord* passes.
11. **No YHWH on a Greek face.**
12. **No bullet may follow a source line.**
13. **Recovery bullets contiguous.**
14. **Panel mirror** — assembled panel matches single-line panel on multiset *and* order; per verse on multi-verse stones.
15. **English witness [v1.4]** — the received English on the page matches the fetched witness, verse by verse (§3.1); on a multi-verse stone, each verse's English sits in its own cycle and nowhere else **[v1.5]**.
16. **Pagination [v1.6]** — keep-with-next on exactly the paragraphs §4.5 names, worked out from the stone's own structure, never typed. **[v1.7]** Headings are found by their place in the order, never by their weight.
17. **Face marks [v1.6]** — each line of the face ends on the mark the stone declares for it, in every cycle and in §2.7 alike, and no dash stands on a face but one the stone declares (§3.8).
18. **A Prayer to Say [v1.7]** — the heading and the line beneath it in their place (§2.8A); the prayer in bold, not italic, at the size of the English face; every line whole on its line in the render, and the block on one page; the face's conventions kept — the Name as the face writes it, *you* in lower case, no emphasis capitals, no quotation marks, no possessive apostrophes, no dash; and the seal still the last paragraph. On a stone that gains its prayer after the seal, every other paragraph of the sealed file is byte-identical.

### 5.1 The scan rule — the one that has failed twice
**Every assert that scans for a fault must run over every source-bearing paragraph in the file.** Never over a named panel.

Twice now a hand has attended to one panel, recorded doing so, and left another untouched — and the record read as though the file were covered. Scope to the file, not to the section you are thinking about.

**What counts as source-bearing. [v1.4]** The single-line source panels, the assembled panels of §2.7, and a round-trip's composition. Recovery bullets quote the source but are not panels: the panel asserts — Latin punctuation, bullet after source — skip them, while the layer check and the replacement-character check run over every character in the file. The first scaffold build failed on a declaration bullet before this was written down.

### 5.2 Every assert must be able to fail [v1.3]
A new assert is run once against a deliberately broken input before it is trusted. An assert that cannot fail on a given stone is reported as **not exercised**, never as passed — the YHWH asserts on Ecclesiastes, which never uses the Name, are the standing example. A vacuous assert was found in a POD build script on 4 October 2026 and removed.

## 6. THE TWO-HAND METHOD

**The eyes and the hand stay separate.**

- **Fable blooms. Opus builds and chisels.** The Free Quarry runs on Opus except for the bloom itself, which is Fable's. The Thirteenth Runner chisels.
- The chisel inspects and cannot write.
- The builder writes and did not inspect.
- Neither can close a loop alone.

A hand can identify a fault and reproduce it in the same breath, because **diagnosis and transcription are different acts**. This is not hypothetical — it happened on the Zephaniah stone.

The cycle is: **chisel → notes → build → chisel.** One artifact push per cycle.

**For Ultra work, multiple sets of eyes are standard.** The bigger work needs more sets of eyes.

**The scaffold. [v1.3]** The Thirteenth Runner builds the scaffold (§2A). The Free Quarry, on Opus 5.5, checks its source panels and transliteration first; only then does Fable bloom. The scaffold's writer never closes its own loop.

**Two hands on the transliteration. [v1.3]** Where the script allows it, the transliteration is derived programmatically from the source and regression-tested against a confirmed stone (Greek). Where it does not (Hebrew), one hand writes it — by hand, or by a transliterator applying Annex A's table — and the build diffs it against a second hand's before it enters a panel. **[v1.4]** Where the hands disagree, the table settles it; where the table is silent, the disagreement is declared and the table extended.

**The two-Fable chain. [v1.7]** The two-hand rule in another form: two instances of the model, as well as two chambers. One brief goes to both Fables, written by the Thirteenth Runner from the stone itself, or for a bloom from its scaffold, carrying its findings and the house rules.
1. Fable in the Free Quarry writes from the brief.
2. Fable in the Thirteenth Runner writes from the brief, blind: the Quarry's text does not enter the chamber until hers is written.
3. The Runner's Fable settles the two, licensed to remaster and not only to refine: keep, merge or rebuild. She says which lines came from which hand and why, and both stay on record.
4. The Runner's Opus chisels the settled text and places it on the stone.
5. The Free Quarry's Opus checks the stone on the repository.

Fable's steps stay as text in the chat, which is what makes the chain affordable. **Where the two hands agree on something the brief supplied, the agreement shows the brief was clear, not that the hands found it apart**; the chain earns its keep where the hands differ and the settlement chooses. It is standard for the prayer to say (§2.8A), and the house's option, stone by stone, for the bloom of a short stone, as Psalm 131 was bloomed. Ruled by the house, 14 October 2026.

## 7. THE NAMING RULE

**Name the recovery, never the famous half.**

Settled across the tablets. *John 8:32* is not *The Truth Will Free You*. *1 Peter 5:6* is not *Under the Mighty Hand*. *Psalm 121:2* is not *Maker of Heaven and Earth*.

Three further constraints:
- **A title cannot hold a fork.** If two readings stand, the name takes one and the note holds both.
- **A title must not point at something absent from the face.**
- **Check for modern collision.** *Fiat* sends the reader to currency. *The Lodger* reads as permanence. *The Loaded* reads as drunk.

---

## 8. STANDING LESSONS

- **Assert on the final artifact**, in the form the reader will see — not on the intermediate the edit touched.
- **Take references from the canonical set in the repo**, never from a recent build of your own. A measurement derived from a broken build and then used to measure everything else is circular.
- **Count tokens explicitly on every quarry pass.**
- **Shelf superlatives are counted against the stone files, never the registers. [v1.3]** Longest, shortest and first claims failed three times in one week — 2 Corinthians 10:5, 1 John 4:19 and Exodus 20:8 — because the registers do not hold the early tablets.
- **The house does not accuse the committees of a fault where they made a reading.**
- **Harmonisation runs both ways.** If the shelf holds the stronger reading, the stone takes it. If the stone finds what the shelf missed, take it and flag the other. **Revise, never propagate.**
- **Young's Literal has repeatedly stood alone and been right.** When YLT departs from the committees, look hard before dismissing it.
- **Where the committees vary most freely, the source usually says least.** When the committees supply different words at the same spot, check whether the source has a word there at all. At 2 Corinthians 9:6 one reads *Remember this* and another *The point is this*; the Greek has no verb.
- **The best-of-both rule.** Where two committees each keep half of what the source holds, the stone takes both halves — one committee's word with the other's consistency, as with *people* three times at Galatians 1:10.
- **A correction pass must know which panel it is standing in.** Punctuation may be cleaned anywhere; words may only be corrected in source panels. A round-trip is evidence, not text.

## 9. WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS NOT

This is not a template. **Do not clone a stone to start a new one.**

Build from this specification, fetch the source fresh, and let the passage decide its own lineation. Two stones built correctly from this document will resemble each other because they follow the same rules — not because one was copied from the other.

The difference is invisible in the output and decisive in the shelf.

## ANNEX A — HEBREW

- **Base:** the Westminster Leningrad Codex, fetched accented (openscriptures/morphhb). Where editions of the same base differ, the panel carries the witness as printed and the difference is declared.
- **Layers:** letters, vowel points, te'amim. The meteg is a fourth layer (assert 5). The maqqef and paseq are part of the text and survive into every panel.
- **Ketiv and qere:** the source panel carries the ketiv as the witness prints it; the transliteration carries the qere as it is read aloud; the recovery bullets declare both.
- **Cantillation belongs to received text only.** Round-trip compositions carry none.
- **Lineation:** atnach first, then the disjunctives; where there is no atnach, the fallback in §3.7. **[v1.3]** In the poetic books the oleh ve-yored stands above the atnach, and both divide (§3.7). **[v1.6]**
- **Two accent systems. [v1.3]** The Decalogue carries two cantillation systems, and the witness encodes the second as an alternative reading. The panel carries the main system; the other is declared in the recovery bullets, as at Exodus 20:8.
- **Paragraph markers. [v1.4]** The petuchah (פ) and setumah (ס) are the Masoretes' paragraph divisions, not words. They are declared in the recovery bullets of the verse they close and never carried in a panel, as at Isaiah 41:13. Inside a passage they may set the strophes (§2A).

  - **Shva.** A silent shva is not written. A vocal shva is written *e*: at the head of a word, under a doubled consonant, after a long vowel, as the second of two shvas, and after a meteg (*she-yireʾu*, Ecclesiastes 3:14).
  - **Dagesh forte** doubles its consonant (*chayyim*, *mibbeli*) — except straight after a hyphen (*ha-shabbat*).
  - **Furtive patah** is written before its letter (*ruach*, *natuaʿ*). The third-person suffix on a plural noun is *-av* (*ʿalav*).
  - **Hyphens** follow the article (*ha-*), a preposition carrying the article (*la-kol*), the conjunction vav (*ve-*, *u-*, *va-*), a preposition pointed with shva (*be-*, *le-*), the relative *she-*, and every maqqef (*et-ha-kol*). **A one-letter preposition before the Name takes the hyphen whatever its vowel [v1.6]** (*ba-YHWH*, Jeremiah 17:7): its vowel belongs to the word read aloud in the Name's place, and the shelf already writes *la-YHWH*. None follows *min* (*mechabbeq*, *millefanav*), and none the vav of wayyiqtol (*vayyomar*).
  - **A one-letter preposition keeps its stop form** where the pointing softens it after a vowel (*be-ʿitto*, Ecclesiastes 3:11, where the bet is rafe).
  - **Capitals:** *Elohim* and proper names; the Name stays *YHWH*.
  - **Punctuation:** in an Ultra it follows the accents — a semicolon at the atnach and at the oleh ve-yored **[v1.6]**, a comma at each lesser break, a full stop at the silluq. A POD's transliteration carries its face's punctuation.

## ANNEX B — GREEK

- **Base:** the critical text. Textus Receptus and Byzantine readings are declared in the recovery bullets, as the extra γάρ was at Galatians 1:10.
- **Layers:** letters, accents and breathings, with the iota subscript. These replace the Hebrew vowel and cantillation checks; the meteg filter does not apply.
- **Orthographic marks are not punctuation.** The koronis and elision mark stay in the source panel and are excluded from the Latin-punctuation assert, as at 2 Corinthians 9:6.
- **The Name:** θεός is *God*; YHWH never appears on a Greek face (§3.8, assert 11).
- **Lineation:** Greek carries no Masoretic accents to break on. Lines break on clause structure — the particles and conjunctions that join clauses — as the Greek POD tablets have.
- **Transliteration. [v1.4]** Derived programmatically and regression-tested against a confirmed stone. House forms: η *ē*, ω *ō*; θ *th*, φ *ph*, χ *ch*, ψ *ps*, ξ *x*; υ *y*, except in the diphthongs *au*, *eu*, *ēu*, *ou*; γ before γ, κ, ξ or χ is *n* (*angelos*); the rough breathing is *h* (*hous*), and an initial ῥ is *rh*; the iota subscript is not written (*en doxē*); accents are not carried; a capital in the Greek stays a capital.
